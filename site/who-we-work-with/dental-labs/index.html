Overview

Built for how you actually work

Software for the businesses around the chair: dental lab management and case tracking, teledentistry platforms, and the HIPAA-aware integrations that connect them to the practices they serve.

Dental labs, teledentistry providers and insurance-adjacent businesses share a problem that practices do not have: their software market is thin. There are far fewer products built for a lab than for a dental practice, and the ones that exist are often dated, rarely integrate with the practice systems their customers run, and were not designed for a business operating at any real scale.

We build for that gap — case tracking through the production workflow, digital case submission from practices, scheduling and logistics, and the integrations that let a lab receive cases and return status without anyone re-keying a prescription. The same applies to teledentistry and insurance-adjacent products where the underlying need is connecting to dentistry properly.

Building for labs and adjacent businesses

What this software has to get right

Case tracking that mirrors the bench. A case moves through defined stages with a due date, a technician, materials and a shipping deadline. Software that models this loosely produces the thing labs hate most: a case whose true status is only known by asking someone.

Digital submission from the practice. Prescriptions, scans and photographs submitted directly rather than arriving as paper in a box. This removes transcription errors on exactly the details — shade, margin design, material — where errors cause remakes, which are pure lost margin.

Status the practice can see. Most calls a lab receives are asking where a case is. Giving practices self-service visibility removes that call volume entirely, and it is usually the feature clients notice first.

Integration where it is possible. Connecting to the practice-management and imaging systems your customers run turns you into part of their workflow rather than a supplier they have to remember to contact — a meaningful competitive position in a market where switching is otherwise easy.

Compliance where it applies. Prescriptions, images and patient identifiers are PHI. Labs are frequently business associates under HIPAA and the obligations are real, so encryption, access control and audit logging are built in rather than assumed.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do you build software for dental labs?

Yes — case tracking, lab management, and the integrations that connect a lab to the practices sending it work. Lab workflows are specific enough that generic tools rarely fit, and integrated enough that a non-dental agency struggles.

Can you build a HIPAA-compliant teledentistry platform?

Yes — with the PHI handling, security, and integrations it requires, HIPAA-aware from day one, connected to the systems that make a virtual visit work end to end.

We're an insurance or benefits business in dental — can you help?

Often, yes. If your software handles dental benefits, eligibility, or claims-adjacent workflows and needs to integrate with practices and clearinghouses, that's squarely in our world.

Why choose a dental-specific team for an adjacent business?

Because your software still has to plug into the dental ecosystem — PMSs, clearinghouses, and PHI rules. A dental-specific team already knows it, so we build integrations that work and avoid the expensive discovery a generalist needs.

We are a dental lab, not a practice. Do you build for us?

Yes. Labs, teledentistry providers and insurance-adjacent dental businesses are a deliberate part of who we serve. The underlying skills are the same — dental workflows, PHI handling, and integration with the practice systems your customers run — and the software market for labs is thin enough that custom is often the only route to what you actually need.

Can practices submit cases directly into our system?

Yes, and it is usually the highest-value piece. Digital prescriptions, scans and photographs submitted straight from the practice remove transcription errors on shade, margin and material — the details that cause remakes — and give the practice self-service visibility of case status, which removes most of the 'where is my case' call volume.
